# Mission observatory

The observatory keeps six measurement programmes scientifically separate. A published JWST spectrum,
a Gaia astrometric source, an HWO accessibility scenario, an ANDES design capability, a future PLATO
light curve, and a Roman microlensing forecast are not interchangeable evidence.

| Mission | Status on 2026-09-12 | Question addressed |
|---|---|---|
| [James Webb Space Telescope](missions/JWST.md) | operational observatory; public atmospheric reductions available | Existing and planned infrared atmospheric characterisation |
| [Habitable Worlds Observatory](missions/HWO.md) | pre-formulation concept and technology maturation | Future reflected-light detection and spectroscopy of terrestrial planets |
| [ELT / ANDES](missions/ANDES.md) | second-phase ELT instrument in development | Future high-resolution spectroscopy, precision radial velocity, and atmosphere pathways |
| [PLATO](missions/PLATO.md) | spacecraft testing; launch planned for March 2027 | Future bright-star terrestrial discovery and stellar characterisation |
| [Gaia](missions/GAIA.md) | Gaia DR3 public; Gaia DR4 not public in this snapshot | Astrometry, distances, stellar context, multiplicity, and future orbital solutions |
| [Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope](missions/ROMAN.md) | launched 2026-08-30; travelling to Sun-Earth L2 and not yet in science operations | Future microlensing demographics across cold, wide-orbit, and free-floating planets |

Every page states what the mission measures, what it cannot establish, its wavelength and resolution
concepts, the data currently available, and the boundary between observation and forecast. Future-release
adapters reject Gaia DR4, PLATO, ANDES, HWO, and Roman science rows as observations until a verified public
release exists.
