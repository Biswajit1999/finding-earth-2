# Finding Earth 2.0 — author vision and roadmap

**Author:** Biswajit Jana
**Status:** author perspective and development plan; not a scientific result

## Why I built this

I began with a simple question: which known world comes closest to Earth? I
expected to build a ranking. Instead, I learned that the real work is deciding
what each number is allowed to mean. A catalogue mixes observations, derived
quantities, model estimates, selection effects and missing measurements. A
single score can hide those differences.

Finding Earth 2.0 became a reproducible evidence system: a route from public
archive records through validation, uncertainty and explicit models to a claim
whose limits remain visible. The research dictionary is part of that system.
It reminds me—and anyone reading the work—whether a result was observed,
derived, model-inferred, simulated, assumed or forecast.

## What I built

- A provenance-tracked catalogue spanning 164,209 source records and 6,354
  confirmed planets, with Solar-System controls kept separate.
- An evidence graph that connects measurements and publications to the claims
  they support.
- A Kepler DR25 selection model and conditional occurrence inference that
  distinguish the visible catalogue from the underlying population.
- Separate composition, climate, stellar-environment, atmospheric, HWO,
  mission, falsification and information-gain layers.
- An interactive research observatory, a generated manuscript, a deterministic
  data release and a public audit trail.
- A Beyond Earth 2.0 chapter that tests extragalactic detection, communication
  delay, relativistic travel and technosignatures against physical limits.

## What I learned

The catalogue is shaped by the telescope. A table value is not always a direct
measurement. Earth-like is a bundle of separate questions. Uncertainty can
change a shortlist. Venus can break an attractive similarity metric. A useful
search should end by asking what observation would teach us the most next.

My conclusion is deliberately restrained: I did not find a second Earth. I
learned how difficult it is to earn that conclusion, and built a system that
makes every step of the search inspectable.

## What I plan next

### 1. Maintain a living evidence base

Keep scheduled archive refreshes reproducible, publish provenance diffs and
make every change in a headline result traceable to a source or model revision.

### 2. Connect the uncertainties

Replace independent approximations with joint stellar-and-planet posteriors
where data support them. Add realistic instrument likelihoods and observing
costs so expected information gain can be compared per unit of telescope time.

### 3. Move from atmosphere scenarios toward evidence

Add target-specific ephemeris covariance, XUV histories and retrieval products
without pooling incompatible reductions. Natural explanations and missing
support should remain attached to every claimed atmospheric signal.

### 4. Grow with future observatories

Version adapters for Gaia DR4, PLATO, Roman, ELT and HWO products when those
data become public. Forecasts will remain forecasts until an archive supplies
observations that can replace them.

### 5. Keep the distant frontier disciplined

Extend the Beyond Earth 2.0 calculations for interferometry, communication and
technosignature searches while treating negative feasibility as a useful result.
Speculation should generate testable questions, never overwrite evidence.

## The direction

The project should become better at saying three things: what changed, why the
conclusion changed, and which observation could change it next. Its north star
is better evidence—not a more confident score.
