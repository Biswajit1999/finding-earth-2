# Finding Earth 2.0 v2 — scientific summary

Finding Earth 2.0 v2 reframes an observed-candidate ranking as a selection-aware
inference and experimental-design problem. The catalogue layer retains the
original 164,209-record funnel: 6,354 confirmed planets, 174 nominally within
the conservative Kopparapu habitable zone, 15 also below 1.6 Earth radii, and
one with an independently measured mass. These counts describe the public
detected-and-confirmed sample only.

The statistically controlled population experiment uses Kepler DR25 GK dwarfs.
It combines transit geometry with a target-aware model calibrated from 146,294
official injections, the observing/pipeline response, vetting, instrumental
reliability, and external astrophysical false-positive probabilities. Synthetic
forward recovery validates flat, power-law, Earth-box, low-completeness,
finite-injection, reliability, and stellar-radius cases and exposes bias under
deliberately wrong completeness and population shape.

Within 50–500 days and 0.5–2 Earth radii, an inhomogeneous Poisson model gives a
conditional integrated rate of 0.692 planets per selected star (95% interval
0.267–1.918). The 89 catalogue candidates correspond to roughly 54 latent valid
candidates and 78.3 shape-weighted effective stars. The Earth-pivot total
selection diagnostic is 1.98e-5, which explains the gap between visible and
intrinsic populations. The result is not a universal eta-Earth: stellar sample,
period-radius box, selection, reliability, and priors are part of its definition.

The candidate analysis is an evidence vector. Independent measured masses gate
two-dimensional composition models. Rogers, Zeng, and Otegi views remain
separate and show material disagreement. MIST-anchored luminosity histories and
three climate boundaries support time-dependent inference for 815 planets, with
22 robustly inside and 16 model-sensitive. XUV activity and 27-case
energy-limited escape scenarios are available only where host and planet support
exists. Atmospheric data preserve 1,826 distinct reductions and 8,309
measurements; clear scale-height scenarios remain separate from detections.

The HPIC v1.1 analysis covers 12,944 HWO precursor stars and a 744-planet
known-host crossmatch. Orbital and instrument uncertainties are propagated under
three declared coronagraph concepts. Gaia, JWST, Roman, PLATO, ANDES, and HWO
remain mission-specific views. Future mission products are forecasts, and
release adapters refuse to present unavailable data as observations.

Expected information gain evaluates six simplified measurement actions for 25
leading candidates. Eighty-three rows meet the data contract; stellar-radius
precision for Kepler-296 f is the largest supported result at 3.94 bits. Costs
and telescope time are not yet included. Solar-System controls provide the most
direct guard against overinterpretation: Venus has ESI 0.874 but fails the
conservative HZ test, while Mars demonstrates that HZ membership is not surface
climate.

The v2.1 objective-conditioned audit shows why those own-parameter scores must
not be read as a universal observing priority. Among 13 targets supporting both
radius actions, stellar-radius EIG is numerically larger in 10 scalar
comparisons, yet it never provides more information about *planet radius* than
a direct radius measurement for `|rho| <= 0.90`. Kepler-296 f transfers 1.19
bits at that ceiling and needs `|rho| = 0.9952` to break even. The correlation
grid is sensitivity analysis because the archive does not publish the joint
posterior.

All results are labelled as observations, model inferences, simulations, or
scenario assumptions. The v2 deposit contains derived products, posterior
samples, query manifests, configurations, and SHA-256 inventories while
excluding provider-controlled source tables. The manuscript's scientific values
are generated from those products. The central conclusion is methodological:
the catalogue is the visible tip of a selected population, and the scientifically
useful next output is often a missing-measurement recommendation rather than a
stronger claim about a planet.
