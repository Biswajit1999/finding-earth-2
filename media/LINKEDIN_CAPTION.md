I asked a simple question: **which known world comes closest to Earth?**

Three days later, it had become a much harder—and much more honest—research
problem.

🌍 What did humanity actually detect?  
🔭 What were our surveys capable of detecting?  
📊 What underlying population could have produced the visible catalogue?  
🧪 Which conclusions survive different composition, climate and stellar models?  
🎯 What should we measure next to reduce uncertainty the most?  
🚀 And if we found a world, could we observe it, contact it—or ever reach it?

**Finding Earth 2.0 v2** now connects 164,209 provenance-tracked archive
records to 6,354 confirmed planets, Kepler DR25 selection and reliability,
population inference, climate evolution, atmospheric survival, HWO imaging
scenarios, expected information gain, and Solar-System falsification controls.

The conclusion is not “I found another Earth.” It is that the catalogue is only
the visible tip of a strongly selected population—and every stronger claim has
to be earned with better evidence.
